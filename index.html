About

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at the Kahlert School of Computing, University of Utah, advised by Dr. Daniel S. Brown. Previously, I received my master's degree from Sun Yat-sen University.

My research interests broadly lie in reinforcement learning and the development of intelligent and reliable agents. My previous work focused on offline reinforcement learning, safe decision-making, multi-objective optimization, and learning from human feedback. More recently, my research explores the robustness of LLM alignment against preference poisoning and the behavioral interpretability of embodied AI systems.

Recent Research
Robustness of LLM Alignment to Preference Poisoning

We investigate how behavioral preference poisoning affects different LLM alignment algorithms, particularly DPO and reward-model-based RLHF. Our research explores the role of reward generalization and reward hacking in shaping policy behavior under poisoned preferences.

Ongoing Research
Understanding Success and Failure in Embodied AI

We investigate the internal representations of vision-language-action models during embodied task execution. Using sparse autoencoders, we uncover interpretable behavioral patterns associated with policy success and failure and explore their utility for early failure prediction.
